Seat: Debord
Verdict: OBJECT
Observed: On index-1280 the answer "9" is the largest, brightest glyph on the screen; READ AS is a small grey 12 px line above it, labelled like a caption. The body is a staged Braun-style object: round keys, one yellow =, a tape with a torn-paper zigzag. The guide table shows "6/2(1+2)" resolving to 9 as settled fact, with no rival reading.
Anchor: Spectacle and recuperation (Society of the Spectacle, thesis 4 and 34): the relation between people mediated by images. The calculator-as-image (nostalgic Rams skin) is the commodity-look, and the answer remains the authoritative spectacle while the exposed reading is demoted to a footnote. Détournement would turn the device's own authority against itself.
Meaning: The site exposes the hidden grammar behind an answer, which is real démontage, but its visual hierarchy still tells you to trust the number.
Artist question: Why does the number keep the throne when the reading is your actual idea?
Neutral question: Do I look at the 9 or at the reading first, and does the page admit another reading exists?
Opinion: Strong idea, tamed by a pleasing skin; the skeuomorph is style doing the spectacle's work.
Requested change: Set READ AS at the answer's size and weight (answer drops one step, dimmer), and for ambiguous inputs (implicit multiplication after a division, leading minus with ^) add a second line "Other calculators may read: 6 ÷ (2(1+2)) = 1", generated by the engine and also shown in the guide row for 6/2(1+2).
