How it reads your maths

Calculators disagree about -2^2 and 6/2(1+2). This one shows you its reading every time. Here are its rules, each with the reading and answer the calculator gave when this page was built. Characters the calculator added are marked; where another common reading gives a different number, it is shown too.

Order of operations

  1. Brackets, and function calls like sin( )
  2. ! and % (after the number)
  3. √ and ∛ (on the number right after them)
  4. ^, right to left
  5. A leading minus
  6. ×, ÷ and a number next to a bracket or constant, left to right
  7. + and −, left to right

Angles

Trigonometry uses degrees until you press DEG to switch to RAD. The screen always shows which. Exact angles give exact answers: sin(180) is 0, and tan(90) is an error, not a huge number.

Numbers and rounding

The calculator works in 64-bit floating point, like JavaScript and most spreadsheets. It shows 12 significant figures and switches to × 10ⁿ from a trillion up and below a millionth (judged after rounding to 12 figures). When the 12 figures round the real value, a ≈ appears and the exact double is printed under the answer.
